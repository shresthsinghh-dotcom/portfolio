My Engineering

My engineering work centers on mechanical system design, prototyping, and testing, with an emphasis on understanding how individual components come together into a reliable system. I am particularly interested in designs that balance performance, safety, and practicality under real-world constraints.

Through both course-based and team-based projects, I have worked through the full engineering design process, from problem definition and concept development to fabrication, testing, and refinement. My approach emphasizes clear decision-making, attention to tolerances, and learning from testing results to improve performance.

Adaptive Ball Launcher: Electromechanical System Design

Project Team

This project was completed as part of EGR 101, a course focused on engineering design and technical communication. I worked closely with a small team to define requirements, divide responsibilities, and coordinate design decisions throughout the project.

Low-Fidelity Prototype

After discussing design goals and constraints, we built a low-fidelity prototype to quickly test initial concepts and gather early feedback. This trebuchet-style launcher allowed us to explore basic launch mechanics, but early testing showed that the design raised safety concerns and lacked sufficient control.

Medium-Fidelity Prototype

The medium-fidelity prototype introduced a more rigid frame and a spring-loaded mechanism, allowing us to better evaluate launch force and trajectory. Testing showed improved consistency and control, and this version served as the foundation for the final design.

Final Prototype

The final prototype integrates a modular frame with low-force activation and an Arduino-controlled linear drive. The system converts electrical input into controlled mechanical motion, allowing users to launch the ball with minimal force while achieving consistent and accurate performance.
